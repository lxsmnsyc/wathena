# Security review, October 2026

This file records the phase 8 step 5 security review (proposal 0002, review decision 51). Each section covers one area of docs/security/threat-model.md. Each finding has its severity, how to reproduce it, and its fix, accepted risk or known issue.

## Scope and method

- **Scope.** The project reviewed itself against docs/security/threat-model.md, in eight areas:
  - protocol and zone,
  - accounts,
  - staff and admin,
  - mods and the Lua sandbox,
  - web,
  - database rights,
  - procedural secrets,
  - deployment and release.
- **Stage 1.** Each area had its own reviewer, which read the code and changed nothing. They found 6 high, 19 medium and 39 low findings, some of them twice. The checking moved some to another severity. "Refuted and partly proven findings" names them.
- **Stage 2.** Checking agents proved or dropped each high and medium finding, most with a test that failed before the fix. Small findings of one area were checked together. There were at most 15 checking agents.
- **Models.** Reviewers and checkers ran on Sonnet.
- **Low findings.** Each was checked again against the code after the fixes of the high and medium findings, then fixed when small, or recorded as a known issue or an accepted risk.
- **Tools.** `govulncheck`, `gosec`, the fuzz tests, `pnpm audit` and `trivy`. See "Tools".
- **Status.** "Fixed" names the commit. "Fixed in the follow-up" is the change after commit eaf4077 that holds the low findings, the connection limits and the zone's open items. "Fixed by decision 54" is the change that built review decision 54 of proposal 0002, which the user approved on 2026-10-19. A known issue is listed in the release notes.

## Staff, accounts and database

Findings of the staff and admin, accounts, and database rights areas. Migration 00038 holds the database changes. Each fix has a test that needs PostgreSQL (`WATHENA_TEST_DATABASE_URL`).

### Sanctions had no rank check (high)

- **Finding.** A sanction checked only that the target was not the actor's own account. A game master could ban every admin and owner, and then no one was left who could lift the bans.
- **Reproduce.** As a game master with a recent login, `POST /admin/api/v1/sanctions` with `{"kind": "ban", "accountId": <admin>, "minutes": 60, "reason": "x"}`.
- **Fix.** `server/adminworld` checks the target's rank before a mute, a jail, a kick, a ban and a lift, from the admin API and the GM commands:
  - Only an owner sanctions an owner, or lifts an owner's sanction.
  - A staff account's sanction needs a role of the actor that has every permission of the target's role. This is `Roles.Covers`, the rule of role changes.
  - The admin API answers `forbidden`. A GM command answers with the reason.
- **Accepted risk.** Staff of the same role can still sanction each other, as they can change each other's roles.
- **Tests.** `TestSanctionRank` in `server/adminworld`: a game master's ban of an admin and of an owner, an admin's ban of an owner, a moderator's mute, jail and kick of a game master and an admin, and a moderator's `@mute` of an admin are refused. A game master's lift of an owner's ban of an admin is refused, and an admin's lift works.

### A 10-year ban needed no `moderation.ban_permanent` (low)

- **Finding.** Only a ban with 0 minutes counted as permanent. A game master could ban for 5,256,000 minutes (10 years) with `moderation.ban` alone.
- **Fix.** A ban longer than a year (525,600 minutes, `admin.LongBanMinutes`) needs `moderation.ban_permanent` too, to give and to lift. A year is the longest mute or jail, so one limit holds for every timed sanction that `moderation.ban` gives alone. A longer ban acts as a permanent one, and review decision 35 gave those to admins.
- **Tests.** `TestSanctionCalls` in `server/admin`: a game master's ban of a year and a minute, and of 10 years, are refused, a year works, and the lift of a two-year ban needs `moderation.ban_permanent`.

### The account role could erase the audit log (medium)

- **Finding.** `delete_old_staff_audit` ran as the account role and took the cutoff from the caller, with a floor of 30 days. The account role also inserts reviews, with any reviewer. So it could review every row marked for review and then delete all rows older than 30 days.
- **Reproduce.** As `wathena_account`: insert a review for each unreviewed row, then `SELECT delete_old_staff_audit(now() - interval '721 hours')`.
- **Fix.**
  - The account role can no longer run `delete_old_staff_audit`. The retention job runs it as the game role, which cannot write reviews.
  - A row marked for review goes only once its review is 30 days old, so a new review never frees a row at once.
  - A trigger gives each review the database's time, and refuses a review of a row not marked for review or by the row's own actor.
- **Accepted risk.** A compromised game role can delete rows older than 30 days, before `audit_days`, as it could drop item log partitions before. A compromised account role can still review rows in the name of another account. The floor stays 30 days, the shortest `audit_days`.
- **Tests.** `TestDeleteOldAudit` and `TestAuditReviewRules` in `server/store`, and `TestJob` in `server/retention`.

### The account role could make the deletion job delete accounts at once (medium)

- **Finding.** `wathena_account` could insert any row in `account_deletions`, and `delete_account` trusted the time the game side passed. A request with `delete_after = now()` was deleted at the next hourly run.
- **Reproduce.** As `wathena_account`: `INSERT INTO account_deletions SELECT id, now() - interval '1 day', now() FROM accounts`.
- **Fix.**
  - The account role loses `INSERT` on `account_deletions`. It calls `request_account_deletion(account, wait)`, which sets both times from the database clock and refuses a wait under 14 days.
  - `delete_account` deletes only a request that is due on the database clock, whose wait was at least 14 days. A later time from the caller changes nothing. `DueDeletions` uses the database clock too, so the job does not kick players whose deletion is not due.
- **Tests.** `TestDeletionWaitInDatabase` and `TestAccountPageRights` in `server/store`, and `TestDeletionJob` in `server/adminworld`, which runs the job with the server clock 15 days ahead and checks that nothing is deleted or kicked.

### GM commands worked on a stale staff session (medium)

- **Finding.** The game ticket recorded only the second factor. A stolen staff cookie from weeks ago could get a ticket and use `@item`, which makes items as a restore does, while a restore needs a login from the last 10 minutes.
- **Fix.**
  - The ticket records whether the session proved who its player is within the reauth window when the ticket was made: `game_tickets.recent`, `store.Ticket.Recent` and `staff.Login.Recent`.
  - `staff.NeedsRecentLogin` lists the permissions whose commands need it: `items.create` and the sanctions. `staff.Login.Allows(permission)` gives the answer, and `staff.ReauthText` the message.
  - The zone checks it in `gmCommand`, after the permission check, and answers `staff.ReauthText`. The dev logins set `Recent: true` too.
- **Accepted risk.** A game session keeps its ticket's answer until the next login, as it keeps the second factor rule.
- **Tests.** `TestTicketRecordsRecentLogin` in `server/account`, and `TestGMItemNeedsRecentLogin` in `server/zone`.

### Staff read whispers and item history without an audit row (low)

- **Finding.** Report details show the target's chat, whispers too. Character logs, item histories and serial searches show what players own and trade. None wrote an audit row.
- **Fix.** These reads write audit rows: `reports.view`, `characters.logs` (the first page of a search), `items.history` and `players.search_serial`.
- **Tests.** `TestReportCalls`, `TestLogsAndHistory` and `TestPlayerSearch` in `server/admin`.

### Full limiter tables locked out every new name (medium)

- **Finding.** When the name table held 100,000 live entries, every name without an entry was refused for a minute, even with the right password. About 100,000 failed logins with made-up names in an hour filled it.
- **Fix.** A full table pushes out the entry used least recently of a sample of 16, preferring entries without attempts in flight. The per-address buckets do the same. A new name is never refused for lack of room.
- **Accepted risk.** A large flood can push out a name's own failure count early. The per-address buckets still limit the flood.
- **Tests.** `TestFullNameTableDoesNotLockOutNewNames` and `TestFullTablesEvict` in `server/account`.

### The per-name login limit was check-then-act (medium)

- **Finding.** The limiter checked a name's failures before the password hash and counted the failure after it. Guesses sent together all passed the check, so a distributed burst got far more than 5 guesses per wait. The same held for TOTP codes across many pending logins.
- **Fix.** `limiter.reserve` counts an attempt as in flight under the lock of the check. Failures and attempts in flight together reach 5 at most. After that, one attempt at a time goes, after the backoff. The login, the TOTP code, the reauth and the TOTP setup use it.
- **Tests.** `TestConcurrentAttemptsAreReserved` sends 200 attempts at once from different addresses and checks that 5 go through. `TestSweepKeepsAttemptsInFlight` checks that a sweep keeps an attempt in flight.

### A victim could not end a thief's session (low)

- **Finding.** No page ended other sessions, no password change existed, and changing login methods left every other session alive.
- **Fix.**
  - The settings page shows how many other sessions are logged in, with "Log out everywhere else". It needs a login from the last 10 minutes. It ends the other sessions, their logins that wait for a code and their unused tickets, and closes their game connections.
  - A password change, on the settings page with the current password, ends the other sessions the same way.
  - Turning TOTP on or off, new recovery codes, and adding or removing a passkey end the other sessions too.
- **Accepted risk.** The page shows a count, not a list of devices.
- **Tests.** `TestLogOutEverywhere`, `TestPasswordChangeEndsOtherSessions` and `TestLoginMethodChangesEndOtherSessions` in `server/account`.

### Other changes

- `deploy/postgres/roles.sql`, deploy/README.md and server/README.md list the new rights.
- The privacy notice says a row that waits for review is kept until 30 days after its review.
- Decision 0049 and strategy section 7.10 still say the account role runs the audit deletion and that a reviewed row goes at once. This section replaces those lines.

## Zone and gateway

Findings of the protocol and zone area. The tests run without a database.

### The Attack command had no PvP check (high)

- **Finding.** The Attack command took any player in view as a target, so a modified client could hit and kill players anywhere. Only summons were checked.
- **Reproduce.** Send `Attack` with another player's entity ID.
- **Fix.** The Attack command takes a target only when `isEnemy` allows it, as rAthena's `battle_check_target`. `fight()` drops a player's target that is not an enemy, whatever set it. PvP maps are not ported, so players are never enemies. Summons keep the `alchemist_summon_setting` rule.
- **Tests.** `TestSecurityAttackCommandRefusesPlayers` in `server/zone`. `TestOverweightRules` now attacks a monster.

### No rate limits, and one shared command queue (high)

- **Finding.** The gateway limited only chat. Every command went into the zone's one queue of 1024. When it was full, the zone refused the next command of any player, and the gateway disconnected that player. One connection could get every player of a map disconnected.
- **Reproduce.** Send `WalkTo` as fast as the socket allows while other players play on the map.
- **Fix.** Each player may have 64 commands in its zone's queue, so a full share refuses only that player's commands. The gateway limits messages per connection and per kind of command (`server/gateway/limits.go`):
  - All messages: 60 at once, then 30 a second. Chat has its own limit instead.
  - Walks: 20 at once, then 10 a second.
  - Attacks, pick-ups, item use, equipment, stats, skills and guild skill ups: 30 at once, then 20 a second.
  - Drops: 10 at once, then 5 a second.
  - NPC clicks, replies, shop commands, and the store search window's pages, clicks and closes: 20 at once, then 10 a second.
  - Storage moves: 30 at once, then 20 a second.
  - Party, friend, guild, trade, mail, store, block, report and channel commands: 20 at once, then 5 a second.
  - Store search queries: 5 at once, then 1 a second.
  - Pings: 10 at once, then 5 a second.
- **Abuse.** A message over a rate, or a command the queue refused, is dropped and counts as a strike. Strikes fall by one every 100 ms. More than 100 end the connection with `DISCONNECT_REASON_RATE_LIMITED`. A client at twice a kind's rate is only slowed. A flood is disconnected within a second.
- **Why these numbers.** The web client sends a walk at most every 100 ms while the mouse is held. The load test bots think every 100 ms and send at most a walk, an attack, a chat line and a ping each time.
- **Changes after the e2e run.** The e2e bots found two kinds that the first rates put in the wrong bucket. A guild master raising 20 guild skills in a row used up the social rate, so guild skill ups count as actions, as `LearnSkill` does. The store search window's clicks and closes used up the search rate, so only a query counts as a search. The duplication bots and the phase 5 exit check's bots act faster than people, so they now keep under the rates with `gateway.Pacer`, which leaves a margin of two tokens and a tenth of the time, since messages can reach the gateway closer together than they were sent. Without it, these bots lost messages and their checks waited out their 3 seconds. A duplication bot asks for a trade at most once a second, and waits a second after `DROP_ITEM_FAILURE_TOO_MANY`. The restore duplication test counts a restore that commits as its run ends. The e2e fixture's Test Charm sets `buy: 200`, since the price rule of eaf4077 refused its `sell: 100` without one. The load test bots do not pace, so their runs show whether the rates refuse normal play. Test: `TestPacerKeepsWithinLimits`.
- **Tests.** `TestOneSessionCannotFillTheSharedQueue` in `server/zone`. `TestCommandLimit`, `TestKindOf` and `TestCommandFloodDisconnects` in `server/gateway`.
- **Follow-up.** Connection limits per address and a rate limit on `Hello` were built after this fix. See "Connection limits per address" below.

### Ground item floods disconnected players (high)

- **Finding.** A player could drop a stack one item at a time with no limit. A player who came into view got every item in its first frame. Over 64 KiB, the gateway refused to send the frame and closed the connection, so players near the pile could not stay connected. Each drop also walked every entity of the zone for each cell it tried.
- **Reproduce.** Drop 3000 single items, then let another player walk into view.
- **Fix.**
  - A player may have 50 of its dropped items on the ground at once. Items fade after 60 seconds.
  - A player drops 10 items at once, then one every 200 ms.
  - A map with 2000 ground items takes no more drops from players. Monster loot is not limited.
  - A refused drop gets the new `DROP_ITEM_FAILURE_TOO_MANY`.
  - The zone counts ground items as they are added and removed, so the checks are cheap.
  - A drop, and a kill's loot, walk the entities once for the cells within 2 of the drop, instead of once per cell tried.
  - A tick's events over 64 KiB go out in several frames of the same tick, each under the limit.
- **Why split frames.** A cap on Appear events per tick would bound only appears. A frame also grows from moves in a crowd, combat, chat and other events. Splitting covers all of them in one place. The client already applies several frames of one tick in order, since the zone sends a vanish frame when a player leaves. A single event over the limit cannot happen with the events there are. It would be logged and dropped.
- **Tests.** `TestDropFloodFirstFrameStaysUnderMessageLimit`, `TestDropLimits` and `TestManyGroundItemsSplitFrames` in `server/zone`.

### Ranged attacks went through walls (medium)

- **Finding.** A basic attack only checked the distance, so an archer could shoot over a wall or a cliff, and farm monsters that could not reach it.
- **Reproduce.** Attack a monster 6 cells away with a wall cell between, with a bow of range 9.
- **Fix.** A target 2 or more cells away needs a clear shot through `path.Shoot`, as rAthena's `battle_check_range` with `path_search_long` and `CELL_CHKWALL`. As `unit_attack_timer_sub`:
  - A player that keeps attacking walks toward the target until the shot is clear.
  - A monster walks toward the target with its chase range 2 less.
  - A single attack gives up.
- **Range.** A player's attack range is a circle, as rAthena's `check_distance_client_bl` in `battle_check_range` and `unit_attack_timer_sub`. A monster's stays square. rAthena builds without `CIRCULAR_AREA` by default (`src/config/core.hpp`), so this is its default rule. Skills already used it. With a bow of range 9, a target 9 and 4 cells away is in range, and one 9 and 9 away is not. The parity tests of the zone stay green.
- **Accepted risk.** rAthena adds 1 to the range when the target walks. wAthena does not.
- **Tests.** `TestBasicAttackNeedsClearShot` and `TestPlayerAttackRangeIsCircle` in `server/zone`.

### Hidden players were sent to every viewer (medium)

- **Finding.** A player under Hiding, Cloaking or Chase Walk was sent to everyone in view, with its option bits. A modified client could draw it.
- **Reproduce.** Hide next to another player, and read that player's frames.
- **Fix.** Such a player is sent only to:
  - itself,
  - its party members, its allies in rAthena's `clif_ally_only` rule,
  - viewers with the detector mode, as `status_check_visibility`.
- Viewers who knew the player get a vanish when it hides, and an appear when it shows again. A GM under `@hide` stays hidden from everyone else, as before. rAthena has no GM exception for hidden players, so none was added.
- **Monsters.** A monster under Hiding, Cloaking or Chase Walk is not sent to viewers it is an enemy of, unless they have the detector mode, as rAthena's `clif_ally_only` and `status_check_visibility`. A player's summon stays visible to players. Viewers who knew it get a vanish when it hides.
- **Tests.** `TestHiddenPlayersAreNotSent`, `TestHiddenPlayersStayVisibleToAllies` and `TestHiddenMonstersAreNotSent` in `server/zone`.

### Trade requests filled another player's frame (low)

- **Finding.** Repeated trade requests from one player added one dialog each to the asked player's frame, with no limit.
- **Reproduce.** Send 4000 trade requests to one player within a tick.
- **Fix.** A player with a request open gets no second dialog from the same asker. An asker reaches at most one player a second. A request too soon gets `TRADE_OUTCOME_FAILED`. rAthena has no such limit.
- **Friend requests.** A player with a friend request open from the same asker gets no second dialog. An asker sends at most one friend request a second. A request too soon is dropped, since the protocol has no failed outcome for friends. rAthena has no such limit.
- **Tests.** `TestRepeatedTradeRequestsDoNotBloatVictimFrame` and `TestTradeRequestsAreThrottled` in `server/zone`, and `TestRepeatedFriendRequestsDoNotBloatTarget` and `TestFriendRequestsAreThrottled` in `server/social`.

### The zone did not recover panics (medium)

- **Finding.** Game logic runs on each zone's goroutine, which did not recover panics. A bug in any command handler would stop the process, every zone, and every unsaved change. No trigger was found.
- **Fix.** The zone recovers every panic and logs it with its stack (`server/zone/recover.go`):
  - A command handler's panic ends the sender's session with the new `DISCONNECT_REASON_SERVER_ERROR`. The zone and the other players go on.
  - A panic while the zone builds one player's frame ends that player's connection. The player then leaves with a normal save, since building frames changes no game state.
  - Any other panic stops that zone: in a tick, a script, a commit, a join or a leave. Its players are disconnected with `DISCONNECT_REASON_SERVER_ERROR`, joins get `ErrStopped`, and other zones go on.
- **Why no duplicates.** A handler that panics may have moved items halfway. So:
  - Nothing the handler queued for the next commit is written.
  - The sender, its trade partner, and the players the handler's transfers changed are tainted. A tainted player's session ends with its last saved copy, never with what is in memory. Its logout handlers do not run.
  - When the handler's commit also held storages, guild storages, mail, shop stock, store rows or event units, or the panic came from inside a transfer, the zone cannot tell which objects changed. It stops, and every player of the zone ends with its last saved copy.
  - A tick's panic always stops the zone, since a tick changes items outside transfers, such as loot that falls.
- **Accepted risk.** After such a stop, a storage, guild storage or mailbox that a player in another zone also holds keeps its state in memory. Personal storages load again from the database once no character holds them.
- **Tests.** `TestCommandPanicEndsOnlySender`, `TestTransferPanicStopsZone` and `TestFramePanicEndsOnlyThatPlayer` in `server/zone`.

### Connection limits per address (follow-up)

- **Finding.** Strategy section 7.4 limits connections per IP address, in place of rAthena's `ddos_*` settings. The gateway had no such limit and no rate limit on `Hello`, so one address could open connections until the server ran out of memory or file handles.
- **Fix.** `server/gateway/connlimit.go`:
  - An address may hold 32 game connections at once.
  - An address may open 20 connections at once, then one every 3 seconds. Each connection sends one `Hello`, so this is the rate of `Hello`s and of guesses at session tokens. It matches the account service's rate for game tickets.
  - A connection over a limit gets HTTP 429 with `Retry-After`, before the WebSocket starts.
  - The address is the client's through `trusted_proxies`, as the account service finds it. The code moved to `server/clientaddr`, which both use. An IPv6 address counts by its /64.
  - Loopback addresses are not limited. They are the host's own tools, such as the load test bots, or a proxy on the same host that `trusted_proxies` does not name. Its players would otherwise share one address.
  - The table of addresses holds at most 100,000 entries, and drops idle ones once a minute.
  - The config keys `connections.per_address`, `connections.hello_burst` and `connections.hello_refill` change the numbers.
- **Why these numbers.** A household or an internet cafe behind one address needs far fewer than 32 connections. The ticket rate already bounds how often a real client can connect.
- **Accepted risk.** A proxy that is not on loopback and not in `trusted_proxies` makes all its players one address, which the limits then refuse after 32. The account service's limits already need `trusted_proxies` for the same reason, and the deploy guide says to set it.
- **Tests.** `TestConnLimitPerAddress`, `TestConnLimitHelloRate`, `TestConnectionsPerAddressThroughProxy` and `TestRetryAfter` in `server/gateway`, and `TestConnectionsConfig` in `server/cmd/waserver`.

## Gateway, web and development mode (low)

### The debug server answered web pages (low)

- **Finding.** The debug address listens on loopback, but it checked neither `Host` nor `Origin`. A page in the owner's browser could send a blind cross-site POST to `http://127.0.0.1:8301`, or use DNS rebinding to read the answers. That reached pprof, the metrics reset and, with `--dev`, the staff, restart, clock and sanction endpoints. Two reviewers found it.
- **Reproduce.** With `waserver --dev --debug-addr 127.0.0.1:8301`, a page posts a form to `http://127.0.0.1:8301/debug/wathena/staff?name=<character>&role=admin`.
- **Fix.** `debugGuard` in `server/cmd/waserver` wraps the debug server:
  - The `Host` must be a loopback name or address. A rebinding page has its own domain there.
  - A request with `Sec-Fetch-Site` other than `none` or `same-origin`, or with an `Origin` other than the debug server's own, is refused. Browsers send these with cross-site requests. curl, Go and Node's fetch send neither, so the e2e tests and the browser checks still work.
- **Tests.** `TestDebugGuard` in `server/cmd/waserver`.

### The WebSocket Origin check ignored the scheme (low)

- **Finding.** The gateway accepted any `Origin` whose host and port matched the `Host` header, whatever the scheme. A DNS rebinding page has its own domain as both. Against a `--dev` server, whose session token is a character name, such a page could play any character. Two reviewers found it.
- **Fix.**
  - The trusted origins are compared with the scheme, as before.
  - A page on the server's own host counts only when the host is loopback and the scheme is the request's, for a development server that serves the client without a public URL.
  - A request without `Origin` is still accepted. It does not come from a browser page, and the ticket is what lets it in.
- **Tests.** `TestOriginAllowed` in `server/gateway`.

### Development mode behind a proxy on the same host (low)

- **Finding.** `--dev` lets anyone play any character. Its only guard was a loopback `listen`, which a proxy on the same host passes.
- **Fix.** `--dev` also refuses `trusted_proxies`, and a `public_url` on a host that is not loopback. A server behind a proxy sets both.
- **Accepted risk.** A proxy on the same host that forwards to a `--dev` server without either setting still makes it public. The guide never runs `--dev` behind a proxy.
- **Tests.** `TestDevRefusesProxySettings` in `server/cmd/waserver`.

### Guild emblem uploads had no rate limit (low)

- **Finding.** A guild master could upload emblems in a loop. Each upload notified every online member and made their clients fetch the new image.
- **Fix.**
  - `postEmblem` checks the session cookie before it reads the body, so a caller without a login gets no parse.
  - It takes a token from the account page's limit per address.
  - A guild changes its emblem at most once a minute (`account.EmblemCooldown`). A second upload in the minute gets 429.
- **Tests.** `TestGuildEmblemLimits` in `server/account`.

## Mods and the Lua sandbox

Findings of the mods and Lua sandbox area. Review decision 52 in proposal 0002 approved the changes to the Lua API. The tests run without a database. Each fix's test was seen to fail before the fix.

### Data overlays changed core prices and drops with no permission (high)

- **Finding.** A data overlay checked only the fixed fields, such as an item's ID. A mod with no permissions could raise a core item's sell price, change a monster's drops or exp, change a quest's drops, or make a reserved staff word plain in the chat filter. `rules.override` covers drop rates and `shops.modify` shop costs, so this went around them.
- **Reproduce.** A mod with no permissions and `data/items.yml` `- {name: Jellopy, sell: 2000000000}` loads.
- **Fix.**
  - The new flagged permission `data.core_override` covers the economy fields of records that another mod added, core's too: an item's `buy` and `sell`, a monster's `drops`, `mvp_drops`, `base_exp`, `job_exp` and `mvp_exp`, a quest's `drops`, and a chat filter word's `action` and `match`. A mod's own records need nothing.
  - `wactl mod validate` and `wactl mod install` list the records such a mod changes, as `items Jellopy: sell`.
  - rAthena's `itemdb.cpp` refuses prices that let a player make zeny with Discount and Overcharge: `buy / 124` below `sell / 75`. It sets `sell` to 1 with a warning. wAthena refuses such a record of a mod with an error. Every core price already follows the rule. Two example items set only `sell` and now set `buy` too, as rAthena would make it: twice `sell`.
- **Accepted risk.** A mod's shop can still sell its own item for less than the item's sell price. rAthena only warns about that in `npc.cpp`, and wAthena does not warn yet.
- **Tests.** `TestOverlayCoreEconomyNeedsPermission`, `TestOverlayCoreOverrideAllowed`, `TestOverlayCoreOverrideQuestsAndChat` and `TestItemPricesCannotMakeZeny` in `server/mod`, and `TestInstallListsCoreOverrides` in `tools/modkit`.

### A kept hook next() ran code in another mod's context (high)

- **Finding.** A hook could keep its `next` function and call it after its chain ended. `next` switched the context by the hook's index in whatever chain ran then. A mod's code then ran with another mod's permissions and environment.
- **Reproduce.** Mod M keeps `next` from a `drop_rate` chain and calls it from its `experience` hook, whose chain has mod V's hook at the same index. M's next `drop_rate` hook runs as V and passes V's `game.read` check.
- **Fix.**
  - A `next` stops working once its hook's call returns. Calling a kept `next` raises "the hook that got this next has returned".
  - Each run of a chain has a token. `hook_enter` refuses any token but the running chain's, so no stale or outer `next` can switch the context.
  - The context that a hook replaced comes back from a stack when the hook returns, instead of from the hook's index.
- **Tests.** `TestStoredHookNextKeepsChainContext` and `TestKeptHookNextRaises` in `server/modscript`.

### One mod could use the whole zone's Lua memory (medium)

- **Finding.** Every mod shares the zone's Lua state, and its 16 MB limit was the state's. A mod that kept memory made other mods' calls fail, and those mods were switched off for failing.
- **Reproduce.** Mod A keeps a 1 MB table on each login. After 16 logins, mod B's death handler fails with a memory error, and after 10 failures B is off in that zone.
- **Fix.** Each mod owns the memory its code makes (internal/script, "Owners of memory"):
  - Each Lua block carries its owner in 8 bytes before it. A block that grows belongs to the mod whose code runs. A freed block goes back to its owner, whoever runs then.
  - Each mod has its own limit, 16 MB by default. A call that would go past it stops with a memory error and counts as that mod's failure. Core, the prelude, and memory no mod made share owner 0, with 64 MB.
  - The state has no limit of its own unless one is set: the owners' limits bound it.
  - `lua.wasm` was built again with wasi-sdk 34. Before the change, the build gave the same bytes as the committed file.
- **Accepted risk.** An object one mod keeps that another mod made counts for the mod that made it. A mod that gives a large table to another mod through `require` pays for it.
- **Tests.** `TestHoardingModCannotDisableOtherMods` in `server/modscript`, and `TestOwnerLimits` in `internal/script` with cgo and with `lua.wasm`.

### No CPU budget across calls (medium)

- **Finding.** Each call had a CPU limit, but a mod could make many calls just under it. 40 calls of 15 ms ran in full and none failed, so nothing stopped the mod.
- **Reproduce.** A login handler with a loop of 3,000,000 additions, and 40 logins in a row.
- **Fix.**
  - The binding counts the CPU units it charges, about one per Lua instruction. The runtime gives each run of code to its mod at each switch of context, also between the hooks of one chain.
  - Each mod has a CPU budget per zone tick, 2,000,000 units by default. Once a mod has spent it, its event handlers and hooks are skipped for the rest of the tick, and its timers wait for the next tick. A refusal is not a failure. The zone logs a warning at most once a minute per mod.
  - Setup functions, item, skill and NPC scripts, conversations, schedules and conditions still run, so a player's action is never lost. Their CPU counts.
  - A manifest may ask for another budget with `budget: { memory_mb, cpu_per_tick }`. The owner sets the default with `mods.budget` and the most a manifest may ask for with `mods.max_budget`: 64 MB and 10,000,000 by default. A mod that asks for more does not load. `wactl mod install` shows what a mod asks for.
- **Why these numbers.** The tests of the 21 example mods use at most 15,000 units in one tick and 760 KB of memory per mod. Core's scripts use at most 8 MB in the converter's and the zone's tests. A loop of 3,000,000 additions takes 6,000,000 units and 15 to 27 ms on the development Mac, so the default lets a mod use about 5 to 9 ms of a 50 ms tick.
- **Accepted risk.** Player actions can spend more than the budget, within the per-call limits and the gateway's rate limits. The count hook charges 1,000 units at a time, so CPU goes to the mod that runs when the count is reached.
- **Tests.** `TestSlowButSuccessfulHandlerIsLimited` and `TestBudgetRefusesUntilNextTick` in `server/modscript`, `TestBudgets` and `TestBudgetChecks` in `server/mod`, `TestModBudgetConfig` in `server/cmd/waserver`, and `TestSpent` in `internal/script`.

### player.status was not checked (medium)

- **Finding.** `start_status` and `end_status` checked only that the script could change the game. A mod with no permissions could start any status on a player, such as one that cannot be avoided, for a long time, or end one. The docs said `player.status` gates this.
- **Fix.** Starting and ending a player's status needs `player.status`. Monsters and NPCs need none, and a preview only computes. The verve example asks for it.
- **Tests.** `TestStartStatusNeedsPermission` in `server/modscript`.

### Mods could pass for players and read private chat (medium)

- **Finding.** `player:whisper` took any `from` up to 24 characters, so a mod could whisper as a player or as staff, such as to ask for a password. Announcements did not say where they came from. The `chat` event gave every mod the text of whispers between players.
- **Fix.**
  - `ChatMessage` has the new field `from_mod`: the ID of the mod whose script sent a whisper or an announcement. Core's lines have none.
  - The client shows a mod's whisper as a server line, `[Mod example.bank] Banker: text`, which opens no whisper tab to answer, and a mod's announcement with the same mark.
  - The `chat` event delivers whispers, party lines and guild lines only to a mod with `rules.override`. The `chat` hook already needed it and saw every line.
- **Tests.** `TestWhisperCannotImpersonatePlayer`, `TestChatEventHidesWhispers`, `TestChatEventPrivateLinesNeedPermission` and `TestCoreWhisperIsNotMarked` in `server/modscript`, `TestAnnouncements` in `server/zone`, and the client's `online.test.ts`.

### Mods reached other mods' storages (medium)

- **Finding.** A storage name with another mod's prefix found that mod's record, so any mod could open, count and take from other mods' extra storages.
- **Fix.** A mod reaches only its own extra storages and core's (core has none). The server's own calls still reach every record. The account's storage and the cart keep the permissions the API reference names: none to open and take, `game.read` to count, and `player.give_items` to give.
- **Accepted risk.** Taking items from a player's own storage or cart needs no permission, as the API reference says.
- **Tests.** `TestStorageRecordOfOtherModIsHidden` and `TestStorageRecordOwnAndServer` in `server/zone`.

### Variables and statuses grew without limit (medium)

- **Finding.** Each `variables.*` declaration added an entry, even for the same variable, and nothing freed it. `statuses.define` and server variables had no count per mod, and one mod could take a player's whole share of 4,096 variables.
- **Fix.**
  - Declaring the same variable again gives the same handle. A mod declares at most 256 variables.
  - A mod's values take at most 1 MiB as JSON on one player, and its server variables at most 256 values and 1 MiB.
  - A mod defines at most 256 statuses.
  - Each limit raises an error that names it.
- **Tests.** `TestRedeclaringVariableDoesNotGrow` and `TestModVariableLimit` in `server/modscript`, `TestModVariableBytesPerPlayer` in `server/zone`, `TestModLimits` in `server/variables`, and `TestDefineLimit` in `server/statuschange`.

### load and memory addresses reached mods (low)

- **Finding.** Strategy section 7.7 says mods have no `load`, but their environment had one. `tostring` and `string.format` showed the heap addresses of tables and functions, which help an attack on C Lua.
- **Fix.** Mods have no `load`. `tostring` shows a table, a function or a coroutine as its kind and a number, such as `table: #12`. `string.format` shows `%s` the same way and refuses `%p`, also as a string method.
- **Accepted risk.** The order of `pairs` over a table with table keys still depends on addresses.
- **Tests.** `TestNoHeapAddresses` and `TestEnvironmentIsolation` in `server/modscript`.

### Mod files were read before their size was checked (low)

- **Finding.** The server read each `.lua` file of a mod and checked the total after. A `.zip` entry can state any size, and the reader sized its buffer from it. Manifests and data files had no limit.
- **Fix.** Each file's size is checked before it is read, and the read stops at the limit: 64 KB for `mod.yaml`, 32 MB for a data file, 8 MB for all scripts, and 4 MB for the locale. `wactl` checks the same.
- **Tests.** `TestZipModLimits` and `TestReadLimited` in `server/mod`.

### Unsigned mod packages install (low)

- **Finding.** `wactl mod install` installs a `.zip` file without a checksum or a signature with only a note. A `.sha256` file next to the `.zip` comes from the same place, so it proves only that the download is whole.
- **Accepted risk.** mods/README.md now says so, and says to compare the checksum with one the author publishes elsewhere or to use `--public-key`. Required signatures are phase 9 work.

### Other changes

- mods/README.md, server/README.md, strategy sections 3.8 and 7.7, internal/script/README.md and the threat model describe the changes. The API reference in docs/generated was made again.
- The threat model's finding 1, per-mod memory limits, is resolved without a Lua state per mod.

## Accounts, staff and database (low)

Low findings of the accounts, staff and admin, and database rights areas. Migration 00039 holds the database changes. The store tests need PostgreSQL.

### Weak passwords passed the list of 90 (low)

- **Finding.** The password check refused only 90 common passwords, names and lengths under 8.
- **Fix.** `server/account/common-passwords.txt` holds 65,414 entries, 612 KB in the binary. It merges the old list with two SecLists files at commit 60e43f4d: the NCSC top 100,000 and the xato top 100,000. Entries are lowercase, and those under 8 bytes or not ASCII are left out. SecLists is MIT licensed, and `common-passwords-LICENSE.txt` has the text.
- **Accepted risk.** It is a list of common passwords, not a full breach list. A breach check service is not used, since it sends data out of the server.
- **Tests.** `TestPasswordProblem` in `server/account`.

### The signup captcha never asked (low)

- **Finding.** Signup asked for the captcha only after 10 failed logins from the address. Signups never counted, so a real captcha provider would not have slowed bot signups.
- **Fix.** The signup form always asks for the captcha. With the default `none`, nothing changes for players.
- **Accepted risk.** No real captcha provider is built, so signups are limited only by the address bucket: 3 at once, then 1 every 10 minutes.
- **Tests.** `TestSignupAsksCaptcha` in `server/account`.

### An owner name with no account (low)

- **Finding.** `staff.owners` names accounts by name. If no account has the name yet, whoever signs up with it first becomes an owner.
- **Fix.** `waserver` logs a warning at start for each owner name with no account. deploy/README.md says to list only names that already have an account.
- **Fix by decision 54.** Owners by account ID:
  - Migration 00040 adds `world.owner_account_id`. `wactl world new` records the first account's ID there, in the transaction that makes the world.
  - The game role reads it with the world row. The account role gets no right on it. `waserver` passes the ID to the staff registry, the admin API and the account service.
  - The owner role follows the ID, whatever the account's name. The owner account cannot change its role on the admin screens or ask for deletion, and its foreign key keeps the database from deleting it.
  - `staff.owners` by name still works, for worlds made before the migration. `waserver` logs a warning at start whenever it is set.
- **Accepted risk.** On a world that still uses `staff.owners`, a listed name with no account can be taken at signup, as before.
- **Tests.** `TestMissingOwners` and `TestOwnerAccount` in `server/admin`, `TestRegistryOwnerAccount` in `server/staff`, `TestNewWorld` in `server/store`, and `TestSanctionRank` in `server/adminworld`.

### The retention status showed database errors (low)

- **Finding.** `GET /retention` showed the raw error of the last run to every `world.read` role, with function names and SQL text.
- **Fix.** It shows "The last run failed. The server log has the error." The job logs the full error.
- **Tests.** `TestRetentionCall` in `server/admin`.

### A restore could mail items to the actor's own account (low)

- **Finding.** `items.restore` did not compare the target character's account with the actor's. A staff member could move any serial to their own character, and needed only a friend to review it.
- **Fix.** `World.Restore` refuses a restore to a character of the actor's own account with `conflict`, as sanctions refuse the actor's own account.
- **Accepted risk.** A restore to a friend's character still works. The audit row and its review show it.
- **Tests.** `TestRestoreToOwnAccount` in `server/adminworld`.

### Audit rows of @recall and @renametown were lost on logout (low)

- **Finding.** The audit rows of `@recall` and `@renametown` went through the staff member's session after the change. A staff member who logged out at once left the change without a row.
- **Fix.** The zone that moves the player writes `@recall`'s row in the same step. `@renametown`'s row goes to the zone where it was typed, not to the session.
- **Tests.** `TestRecallAuditSurvivesLogout` and `TestRenameTownAuditSurvivesLogout` in `server/zone`.

### A passkey without user verification counted as a second factor (low)

- **Finding.** User verification is only preferred. A passkey login without a PIN or a fingerprint gave the factor `passkey`, which counted as the second factor for staff roles and for the reauth window.
- **Fix by decision 54.**
  - Migration 00041 adds the session factor `passkey_verified`.
  - A passkey login whose signed assertion has the user verification (UV) flag records `passkey` and `passkey_verified`. One without the flag records `passkey` alone.
  - A staff role counts only for a session with `totp`, `recovery_code` or `passkey_verified`. This holds for the admin API and for game tickets.
  - Sessions made before the migration keep their factors, so a staff member whose session has `passkey` alone logs in again.
- **Accepted risk.** A reauth with a passkey without the flag still refreshes the 10-minute window on an account without TOTP, as a password does. The session's staff role still needs the second factor of its login.
- **Tests.** `TestStaffNeedsVerifiedPasskey` and `TestPasskeys` in `server/account`.

### SECURITY DEFINER functions searched pg_temp first (low)

- **Finding.** The functions set `search_path = pg_catalog, public`, and PostgreSQL then searches `pg_temp` first. A role with `TEMP` could make a temporary `account_deletions` table, and `delete_account` deleted an account that had asked for nothing. `roles.sql` revokes `TEMP`, but a hand-made database or a managed one may keep it. The test databases keep it, and the attack worked there.
- **Fix.** Migration 00039 sets `search_path = pg_catalog, public, pg_temp` on every SECURITY DEFINER function: `ensure_log_partitions`, `delete_account`, `request_account_deletion`, `delete_old_staff_audit`, `check_staff_audit_review` and `drop_old_log_partitions`.
- **Tests.** `TestDefinerSearchPath` and `TestDefinerIgnoresTempShadow` in `server/store`.

### ensure_log_partitions took any date (low)

- **Finding.** The game role could make month partitions for any date, thousands of them, which slows the logs and fills the catalog.
- **Fix.** Migration 00039 refuses a missing day, and one more than 45 days from the database's `now()`. The store passes the current time.
- **Tests.** `TestLogPartitionsBounded` in `server/store`.

### The game role can drop log months older than 30 days (low, accepted risk)

- **Finding.** `drop_old_log_partitions` takes the cutoff from the caller, with a floor of 30 days, while `item_log_days` is 180 by default.
- **Accepted risk.** Decision 0049 accepted it. The period is in the server's config, which the database cannot read. Enforcing it needs a setting only the migrator can change, which is left for a later migration.

### The account role can make characters with any values (low, known issue)

- **Finding.** The account role inserts characters and inventory rows with every column, so a compromised account role can make characters with any zeny and items, with no item log line.
- **Known issue.** Column grants do not help, since character creation sets every start value from the config. The fix is a SECURITY DEFINER `create_character` that sets the start values and logs the start items, which needs the start config in the database.

See "Deployment and release".

## Zone (low)

### Storage stayed open after walking away or dying (low)

- **Finding.** A personal or guild storage closed only on a map change or logout. A modified client could keep moving items from anywhere on the map, in a fight, or while dead.
- **Fix.**
  - A walk or an attack while a storage is open closes it, sends `StorageClose`, and then goes on. rAthena refuses the walk instead, with `pc_cant_act`, but the web client lets the player walk with the window open.
  - A dead player gets `STORAGE_OUTCOME_BUSY` for a move in or out of a storage. rAthena does not check this.
- **Tests.** `TestStorageRefusesDeadPlayer` and `TestWalkClosesStorage` in `server/zone`.

### A buying store bought more than it wanted (low)

- **Finding.** A sale checked each slot against the store line alone. Two slots of the same item could each sell the line's full amount.
- **Fix.** A sale adds up the amount per store line across slots, and refuses a slot that passes it. rAthena's `buyingstore_trade` has the same bug.
- **Tests.** `TestBuyingStoreSaleCountsSlotsTogether` in `server/zone`.

### A stale session can commit after a second login (low, fixed by decision 0054)

- **Finding.** A second login waits at most 10 seconds (`KickWait`) for the old session to leave. An old session still in its zone after that can commit a trade, a sale or a mail. The commit writes the other party's side and skips the stale character's.
- **Reproduce.** `TestLoginWaitsForOlderSession` with `Store.Load` for the second login: the old session gives 100 zeny away after the second login read the character, and the two characters hold 1,100 zeny, from 1,000.
- **Fix.** `Store.Login`, which `waserver`'s logins use, waits until every earlier session of the character ended: its final save is written, or it went stale. `gateway.LoadWait`, 30 seconds, bounds the wait, and a login that gives up is told to try again. The gateway's memory characters wait the same way. A commit with a stale entry still writes the rest: failing it as a whole would leave the other party's items in memory, which its later saves write.
- **Tests.** `TestLoginWaitsForOlderSession` in `server/store` and `TestMemoryLoginWaitsForOlderSession` in `server/gateway`.

### A restore whose request ended still moved its items (medium, found after the review)

- **Finding.** `Zone.do` returned the caller's context error while the zone still ran the call later. A restore whose request ended in the zone (`POST /restore`, or the dev endpoint) then ended its offline holder's session with the unchanged copy and let go of its hold, storages and mailboxes. The zone moved the items into the mail afterwards, and the commit dropped the ended session. The items were in the holder and in the mail. It needs a request that ends while the zone works on the restore, such as a timeout on a stalled machine. It explains the extra Jellopy of `TestDuplicationRestoresWithDatabase` on 2026-10-19.
- **Reproduce.** `TestRestoreThatTimesOutInItsZone`: `jellopy 11, was 10` before the fix. `TestDuplicationCutRestoresWithDatabase` failed 3 of 6 runs before the fix, two with `item 909: 241, was 240`.
- **Fix.** Zone calls from other goroutines are all or nothing (`Zone.call`): a call whose caller gave up before it started never runs, and a started call is waited for. A restore that happened answers with its mail.
- **Tests.** `TestCallIsAllOrNothing` in `server/zone`, `TestRestoreThatTimesOutInItsZone` in `server/adminworld`, and `TestDuplicationCutRestoresWithDatabase` in `e2e`.

- **Tests.** `TestSpawnHooksSecretStream` in `server/zone`.

### node:hash on a secret node could be inverted (low)

- **Finding.** `node:hash(x, y)` on a layer's secret node was rng-v1's 64-bit hash, an XOR fold of the key and steps that can be inverted. A layer that places objects by hash published bits of it.
- **Fix by decision 54.**
  - `node:hash(x, y)` on a generation layer's node is rng-v1's secret hash: the first 8 bytes of HMAC-SHA256 of `"wa-secret-hash-v1" || BE64(x) || BE64(y)`, keyed by the node, read big-endian. It is still a 64-bit Lua integer.
  - The secret hash is `Node.SecretHash` in `shared/rng`, in the file that TinyGo builds leave out, so `gen.wasm` and the client never have it. `gen.wasm` is byte-identical.
  - Public nodes keep rng-v1's hash, so the client and the server still agree on terrain.
  - No version gate: generation runs once, at world creation, so existing worlds keep their stored content. Only mods that hash secret nodes in new worlds see other values.
- **Tests.** `TestSecretHash` and `TestVectors` in `shared/rng`, with new `secret_hash` vectors checked by a separate HMAC implementation, and `TestGenerationHashIsSecret` in `server/modscript`.

### Future seasons' terrain can be computed (low, accepted risk)

- **Finding.** A season's field node is `child(field node, "season", n)`, and the client holds the field node. With the few biomes, a client can compute each future season's terrain.
- **Accepted risk.** Spawns stay secret, since they come from the season key. Changing the derivation would change the golden seasons.

### A forced rotation could run ahead of time (low)

- **Finding.** Each forced rotation moved the active date one step past the last. Forcing it again and again put a dungeon days ahead and stopped its own rotation. The audit row was written after the change was planned, so a failed write left a change with no row.
- **Fix.**
  - `Manager.Force` runs the audit write before it plans anything. A failed write plans nothing.
  - A dungeon already ahead of its schedule cannot be forced again until real time reaches its date (`rotation.ErrAhead`, `conflict` in the admin API).
- **Tests.** `TestForceAhead` and `TestForceAuditFirst` in `server/rotation`, and `TestForceChangeAuditFails` in `server/adminworld`.

### Map info is public for every map (medium, accepted by design)

- **Finding.** `/gen/maps/<id>.json` needs no login and lists, for every map, its name, level band and places, also the places that mods' generation layers drew from their secret nodes. The world map shows names and bands only for visited maps (decision 0041).
- **Checking.** The checker of this finding could not reach the repository and left the default verdict "refuted". It was checked again for this report by reading the code. It is real.
- **Accepted by design.** Review decision 54 keeps `/gen/maps` public:
  - Map names, bands and places are visible in play, and decision 0044 made them public.
  - The client needs the objects to draw a map, and `wabots` reads the same files.
  - Spawns and loot are not in it.
  - A mod that must hide a place should not return it from its generation layer.

## Deployment and release (low)

The Compose changes were checked with the smoke test. Colima's port forwarding is broken on the development machine (decision 0048), so the run skipped only the test's HTTPS requests from the host.

### The backup service held the owner's password (low)

- **Finding.** The long-running `backup` service read `migrate_database_url`, the role that owns every table, also the audit log. `pg_dump` needs only read rights. Two reviewers found it.
- **Fix.**
  - The new role `wathena_backup` is a member of `pg_read_all_data`, with `CONNECT` on the database. `roles.sql` makes it, and `init-db.sh` makes it on every `up`, so running stacks get it too.
  - `secrets.sh` makes `backup_password` and `backup_database_url`. The `backup` service gets only that URL and the recipients.
  - `wactl world backup` reads `--database`, then `WATHENA_BACKUP_DATABASE_URL`, then the migrator's URL.
  - Restores and migrations still run as the migrator, in the `wactl` service.
- **Tests.** `TestBackupURL` in `tools/wactl`. The smoke test checks that the backup service has no migrator secret, and makes, verifies and restores a backup.

### waserver did not check its database roles (low)

- **Finding.** `waserver` only checked that both database URLs were set. An owner who gave it a superuser or the migrator got a server with every right, and no warning.
- **Fix.** `checkDatabaseRole` refuses a pool whose role is a superuser, can make roles or databases, bypasses row security or replicates, or owns tables. The game pool must not read `account_passwords`. With `--dev` it only warns, since the tests connect as a superuser that sets the game role.
- **Tests.** `TestCheckDatabaseRole` in `server/cmd/waserver`.

### Asset builds ran next to the migrator's password (low)

- **Finding.** The guide built assets from GRFs and art packs, which are untrusted input, in the `wactl` service. That container had the migrator's URL and the database network.
- **Fix.** The new `offline` service runs the `wactl` image with `network_mode: none`, no secrets and the assets volume. The guide and the smoke test build assets in it.

### The Docker build context held the kRO client (low)

- **Finding.** `.dockerignore` did not list `data/` or `dist/`. The build's `COPY . .` sent the local kRO client and release output to the daemon and the build stage's layers, though not to the final images.
- **Fix.** `.dockerignore` lists `data`, `dist`, `generated`, `bin`, `backups`, `**/*.age`, `**/*.dump`, `**/secrets`, `**/*.pem`, `**/*.key` and `**/.env*`. The top-level patterns leave the packages' own `data` folders in.

### Caddy ran as root and PostgreSQL kept its capabilities (low)

- **Finding.** The Caddy container ran as root, and the PostgreSQL container kept Docker's default capabilities.
- **Fix.**
  - Caddy runs as 65532 with only `NET_BIND_SERVICE`, which its binary's file capability needs. A one-shot `caddy-init` service changes the owner of the existing certificate volumes.
  - PostgreSQL runs as `postgres` with `cap_drop: ALL`, read-only and `no-new-privileges`.
- **Tests.** The smoke test checks Caddy's user and that PostgreSQL has no effective capabilities.

### Secret files were readable by any user of the folder (low)

- **Finding.** `secrets.sh` made the files mode 644 and relied on the 0700 folder. The systemd example gave no mode for its environment file and no sandbox.
- **Fix.**
  - `secrets.sh` refuses to run when `secrets/` is not a folder of the user with mode 0700.
  - The systemd example sets `secrets.env` to root and 600, and adds `NoNewPrivileges`, `ProtectSystem=strict`, `ProtectHome`, `PrivateTmp`, `PrivateDevices`, the kernel protections, `RestrictSUIDSGID` and `LockPersonality`. `MemoryDenyWriteExecute` is left out, since builds without cgo compile WebAssembly at run time.
- **Known issue.** The files stay 644. Compose without Swarm ignores a secret's owner and mode, and the PostgreSQL entrypoint drops extra groups when it changes user. The guide says to keep the folder's mode when copying the stack.

### The client build trusted the registry for pnpm (low, partly fixed)

- **Finding.** The Dockerfile installed pnpm without a hash. `pnpm audit` reported a high advisory in `source-map-js`, a build tool. Nothing ran `pnpm audit`. `scripts/release.sh` does not check its tools' versions.
- **Fix.**
  - The Dockerfile fetches pnpm 12.7.0 with `npm pack` and checks its SHA-256 before it installs it.
  - `source-map-js` is 1.2.2 in the lockfile. `pnpm audit` finds nothing.
  - The `security` step of `scripts/check.sh` runs `pnpm audit --audit-level high`.
- **Known issue.** `scripts/release.sh` checks only that Go, zig and pnpm are on `PATH`, and `SHA256SUMS` does not prove where the binaries came from. Signed releases and the SBOM are decided work. (2026-10-20: the project publishes no binaries, so there is nothing to sign. Owners build from the release's tag.)

### Database traffic in the Compose network is not encrypted (low, known issue)

- **Finding.** Services connect with `sslmode=disable`, and `init-db.sh` sends role passwords in `ALTER ROLE` statements.
- **Known issue.** The `db` network is `internal: true`, and only the stack's containers are on it. TLS for PostgreSQL and password verifiers made in `init-db.sh` are left for later. The guide's "Security of the stack" says so.

### Backups are not signed (low, known issue)

- **Finding.** age encrypts backups to a public recipient, and the manifest beside each backup is not signed. Anyone who can write the backups folder, or a copy of it, can make a backup that verify and restore accept.
- **Known issue.** Signing manifests with a key kept with the identity is left for later. The guide says to restore only copies the owner made, kept where others cannot write.

## Refuted and partly proven findings

No finding was refuted after a check. These had a verdict other than "proven":

- **`/gen/maps` publishes secret places (medium).** The checker could not reach the repository, and its verdict "refuted" was only the default for a finding it did not check. A check for this report found it real. It is accepted by design (review decision 54): see "Map info is public for every map".
- **Whispers and item history read without an audit row (medium, partly proven).** Reports show whispers by design (decisions 0018 and 0030), and no threat model row said the reads were audited. The checker kept the missing audit trail, as a low finding, which commit 88149d2 fixed.
- **Unbounded variables and statuses (medium, partly proven).** The test proved the growth of `variables.declare`. The limits of statuses and server variables were confirmed by reading only. Commit eaf4077 fixed all of them.
- **Mods reach storages (medium, partly proven).** Reaching another mod's storages was proven and fixed in eaf4077. Taking items from the player's own storage or cart with no permission is what the API reference states, so it stays as designed.
- **A victim's frame bloated by requests (medium, checked as low).** The frame passed 64 KiB only with about 4,700 trade requests in one 50 ms tick, more than the zone's queue takes. The gateway's rates and the trade and friend throttles now stop it.
- **Panics in the zone (medium, checked as low).** No trigger was found, so it could not be shown with a test. Commit 9fd99ca added the recovery anyway.
- **Sanction rank, the audit rights, the deletion job, the stale staff session, the per-name limit and a victim's sessions.** Real, but the checkers ran without PostgreSQL. The fix commits added tests that need it, and they pass.
